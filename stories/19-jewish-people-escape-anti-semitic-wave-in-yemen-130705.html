19 Jewish People Escape Anti-Semitic Wave In Yemen

March 28, 2016 World

In its latest covert operation, a pro-Israeli organization recently helped 19 Jewish people escape from their homes in Yemen, where they evaded the latest violent anti-Semitic tide that has escalated throughout the country in recent years.

The Jewish Agency for Israel helped smuggle the 19 Jews —14 from the Yemeni town of Raydah and five from Sana’a — in its most recent effort to rescue Jewish Yemenite citizens from militant Muslims within the predominantly Islamic nation.

So far, approximately 200 Jewish people in Yemen over the past several years have been rescued by the Jewish Agency for Israel, which has worked to relocate them to safety in Israel, United with Israel reports.

Similar to the ongoing conflict going on in the Middle Eastern nation of Syria, a civil war has also swept over Yemen, which has resulted in increased violence against the Jewish people living there.

Continuing the millennia-old Jewish-Muslim struggle, militant Islamic Yemenites murdered the father of a husband from Sana’a. The husband was smuggled to Israel along with the 18 others. The remains of the father were taken in the rescue effort and transported to Israel, where they will be buried.

Also included among the 19 Jews smuggled from Yemen was a rabbi who toted an ancient Torah scroll with him — a piece of history that dates back to the 13th of 14th century A.D.
