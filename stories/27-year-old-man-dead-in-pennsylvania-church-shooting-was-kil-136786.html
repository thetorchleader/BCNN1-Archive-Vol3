27-Year-Old Man Dead In Pennsylvania Church Shooting was Killed After a Dispute Over Seating

April 29, 2016 Christian

Robert Braxton was killed in a Pennsylvania church on Sunday after an argument over a seat. (Photo Credit: Facebook)

The house of the Lord is supposed to be a place of refuge and safety, but a man was killed at a Philadelphia church Sunday.

A churchgoer fatally shot a fellow attendee after a fight broke out over a seat in the sanctuary, according to authorities.

The dispute began when Robert Braxton III, 27, chose to sit in a place where seats were reserved for two other members at Keystone Fellowship Church, district attorney Kevin Steele said.

When someone tapped him on the shoulder and notified him that those seats were taken, Braxton yelled out, “don’t f—ing touch me,” an affidavit obtained by NBC Philadelphia said. He only calmed down after speaking to a church usher and a pastor said witnesses.

But it wasn’t over.

Things escalated when churchgoer Mark Storms, 46, came over to Braxton, demanding that he leave and flashing his Concealed Weapons Permit badge which the district attorney’s office believes he purchased online.

Mark Storms, 46, flashed a concealed carry badge before opening fire on Braxton, killing him. (Photo Credit: Montgomery County)

“When he came over, he had a gun out, escalating the situation,” Steele said at a press conference Thursday. “Braxton then took a swing at the defendant, punching him in the jaw.”

Witnesses remembered Braxton yelling, “F— you and your fake badge, get the f— out of here.” Another witness said, the unintimidated young man told Storms, “That’s not a real gun.”

According to court records, before punching the shooter, Braxton asked, “What are you going to do, shoot me?” That’s when the gunman opened fire, striking the victim in the chest and right arm.

Braxton was rushed to Abington-Lansdale Hospital, but died from his wounds. The shooter was arrested and has been charged with voluntary manslaughter and reckless endangerment.

“Storms’ shooting of Mr. Braxton was not a reasonable self-defense situation,” the district attorney said. Steele also said Storms had no reason to bring a loaded gun to a crowded church on a Sunday morning—which reportedly draws 250 to 300 people each Sunday.
