29 People Killed in 3 Months of Homicides in Jacksonville, Florida

April 4, 2016 Black

dan.scanlan@jacksonville.com Homicide detectives gather outside a home on Evelyn Drive where the body of a man shot to death was found on Dec. 14.

Bullets ripped through the walls of Jacksonville homes, the steel skins of cars, between burglar bars, across parking lots and residential streets, taking a majority of the 29 lives lost to homicide in the city during the first three months of 2016. 

Fatal violence in the first quarter of the year took many paths, including domestic confrontations, targeted killings and the random deaths of victims whose lives were just starting or heading toward sunset.

Cases tracked by the Times-Union also included beatings and stabbings, but 25 were the result of gunfire — from 22-month-old Aiden Michael McClendon who was in a car when shot to 70-year-old Fredrick Leon Cuffie, who was shot through a locked, burglar-bar door.

Those accused of murder also spanned the generations, from a 15-year-old charged in cabbie Melvin Gene Wright’s fatal shooting to a 61-year-old whose wife was beaten to death.

The 15-year-old was charged with two older teenagers, including his brother, in the death of Wright, 62.

Billy Ray Martin said his 61-year-old wife, Lou Ellen Martin, had “entities” inside her that had to be beaten out, police said at a briefing on his arrest.

Aiden was collateral damage in a gang conflict, police said, while a motive in Cuffie’s slaying at his home hasn’t been released.

While the city was appalled at the 15 homicides in January, by the end of March the pace of killing had evened out when compared to past years. There were 21 in 2015, 29 in 2014, and 26 in 2013 in the first three months of those years.

There have been worse years.

A decade ago in 2006, 44 people were killed by the same time, and in 2007, 34 had died, according to Times-Union records.

There have also been dips.

In 2010, 17 were killed, in 2011 the toll was 18 and in 2012 there were 21.

Sheriff Mike Williams and former Sheriff John Rutherford have pointed to those years as a time when the Jacksonville Journey, an anti-crime effort that tried to address root causes of crime, showed the best results.

Williams last week told a group responsible for providing training advice to the Sheriff’s Office that in 2011 there were 71 murders in the city (murders don’t include justifiable deaths; homicides do). It was a time when the Journey had been having an impact for three or four years.

“We hadn’t had a number that low since 1970,” he said. While crime was down nationwide during that period, Williams said Jacksonville saw a greater reduction.

“The overall crime rate in the country had been down, but it was not down that much,” he said.

A revised version of the Journey, which faded due to a lack of money, is being launched with the backing of Mayor Lenny Curry and the city.

January, the most violent of the first three months of this year, brought the city’s homicide record into sharp focus.

Its most chilling death was of toddler Aiden as he sat in a car with his mother and grandmother outside a Spearing Street house. When police announced the arrest of a 16-year-old in the case a little more than a month later, they said the drive-by shooting had roots in a beef between two Jacksonville street gangs that erupted over rap songs.

The spray of gunfire was intended for Aiden’s 19-year-old cousin who was in the yard of the house, police said.

Aiden was the casualty in one of a handful of shootings that appeared to have other targets. As with the youngster, some people were seemingly in the wrong place at the wrong time.

The day before Aiden’s Jan. 29 death, Jerry Renaldo Brown Jr., 22, was shot in a drive-by outside an aunt’s house as he sat in his car and was using his phone. Brown, who had been at church choir practice earlier in the evening and worked at a cousin’s restaurant, may have been killed in a case of mistaken identity.
