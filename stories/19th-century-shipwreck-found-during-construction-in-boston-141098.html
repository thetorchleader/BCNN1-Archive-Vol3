19th-Century Shipwreck Found During Construction in Boston

May 28, 2016 National

The Seaport District is no stranger to boats, or to construction sites amid the current development boom.

But last week, the two collided.

Boston Archaeologist Joe Bagley said this week that a 19th-century shipwreck was discovered at the site of an ongoing construction project in the waterfront neighborhood, and crews have halted work on the job so that experts can excavate the ship’s remains in hopes of finding out more about its origins.

Bagley has been posting photos of the ship to the city archaeology department’s Facebook and Twitter accounts, keeping followers afloat on their findings.

His photos show archaeologists excavating by hand the remains of lime barrels in the hold of the 19th-century ship, and inspecting its bow.

Charley Leatherbee, an executive with Skanska, the company building at the site at 121 Seaport Boulevard, said in a statement to the Globe that during normal excavation operations, crews discovered “something unusual” in the ground.

“We immediately halted our excavation operations in the area of the site, and alerted the city archaeologist and Massachusetts Historical Commission,” Leatherbee said. “We are working closely with both organizations to determine exactly what this structure is and if it has historical significance so we can work with the city to take care of it in the most respectful manner.”
