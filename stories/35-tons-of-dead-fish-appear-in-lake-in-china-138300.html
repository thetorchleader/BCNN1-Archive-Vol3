35 Tons of Dead Fish Appear in Lake in China

May 8, 2016 World

Dead fish are cleared from the Fuhe river in Wuhan, in central China’s Hubei province on September 3, 2013 after large amounts of dead fish began to be surface early the day before. According to local media, about 30 thousand kilograms of dead fish had been cleared by late September 2. The official Wuhan municipal government’s emergency office Weibo account announced on September 3 that the fish had died of severely high levels of ammonia. (PHOTO CREDIT: STR/AFP/Getty Images)

At least 35 tons of dead fish appeared in a lake in southern China, leaving residents stunned.

The piles of fish washed up in a lake in Hainan province on Wednesday, Chinese state media reported.

Residents expressed concerns on pollution, but local authorities said the fish died as a result of salinity change.

The change in salinity levels likely occurred after a tide pushed the fish up farther into Hongcheng Lake in the city of Haikou, state-run People’s Daily reported.
