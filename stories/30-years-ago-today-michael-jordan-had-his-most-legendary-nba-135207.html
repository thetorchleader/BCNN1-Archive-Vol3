30 Years Ago Today Michael Jordan Had His Most Legendary NBA Game

April 20, 2016 Sports

Michael Jordan supplied a dozen dump trucks’ worth of unforgettable moments through his magnificent 15-year NBA career. Outside of copying and pasting his entire Wikipedia page, there isn’t much more that can be said about all the brilliant seasons, months, games and singular moments His Airness has provided. 

One night stands out. Thirty years ago today, on April 20, 1986, Jordan’s legend was unofficially officially born as an NBA sophomore when the Chicago Bulls star almost singlehandedly slayed the unbeatable Boston Celtics — in the suffocating Boston Garden, no less — in Game 2 of a first-round series:

Jordan scored 63 points on 41 field goal attempts. None were behind the three-point line. He shot 53.7 percent from the floor, dragging his Chicago Bulls through regulation and two overtimes before they lost by four:

This wasn’t Kobe Bryant taking 50 shots against a disinterested Utah Jazz team on the final day of the regular season. This was the best player who ever lived firmly jumping on the track to legendary, in the most impressive way.

This was a 22-year-old superhero coming off a broken foot that October, zooming into the second playoff series of his life. His most talented teammate was Orlando Woolridge (who supported Jordan with a 9-of-27 performance in Game 2).
