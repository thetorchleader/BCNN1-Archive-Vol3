13 African-American Women Share Why Their Church Hats Are An Expression of Faith and Identity

March 27, 2016 Black

Dorothy Rayford is a deaconess at Greater New Hope Baptist Church in Monroe. (Photo: Hannah Baldwin/The News-Star)

Verse 15 from chapter 11 in Paul’s first letter to the Corinthians reads that a woman must cover her head in order to pray or prophesy. It’s a belief shared by the thirteen women who posed for this project. Many of the women talked about dressing for church as a way of expressing respect for God. But their hats are also an expression of personality.

“We are supposed to dress conservatively but my hats are one of my identities. I am known for my hats,” Dorothy Rayford, who is a mother of the church at Greater New Hope Baptist Church in Monroe, said.

Church hats are also a testament to the women’s history. All of them said they grew up with mothers, aunts and grandmothers who wore hats to church. Some of the women started wearing hats in their teens or early 20s – as soon as they were allowed to. Others started wearing hats to church later on, as faith began to play a bigger role in their lives. The women’s personal histories are set against their cultural history.

All of the women in this project are African-American. Hats and headdresses play a significant, often symbolic role, throughout the African Diaspora, wrote Mary Jo Arnoldi and Christine Mullen Kreamer, who co-curated an exhibit for the Smithsonian Institution in the mid-1990s. Hats became a way for African-American women to express individuality and creativity during slavery and under the Jim Crow laws. That connection to her heritage is one reason why Earlean Knox said she wears hats to church. “Something about Black folks, it seems that creativity is in our DNA and I think it’s because you can’t do this, you’ve got to make do, make something work,” Knox said.

Derunzia Atkins, Greater New Hope Baptist Church, Monroe

“I wear hats because when I grew up all ladies, when they became a certain age they wore hats on Sunday. That was the day that everyone looked their best and going to the house of worship. Sunday was a special day for everybody. Back then women even covered their hands. They wore gloves. Now we don’t wear gloves, we don’t wear hose… But on Sunday everybody put on their best. And we were taught that a woman was supposed to have her head covered but I read later in the Bible that it was talking about her hair. We were reared to think that a woman was not supposed to go to church without her head covered with a hat. When I came along, it’s been a long time ago, but the women when they came of age all the women had on hats. They wore hats to church because Sunday was a special day and the house of worship was a special place for us.”
