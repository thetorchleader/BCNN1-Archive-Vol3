10 Students Receive College Scholarships In Honor of Slain Emanuel AME Church Pastor Rev. Clementa Pinckney

June 1, 2016 Black, Education

Ten students, including six from Charleston County, will receive about $80,000 total this year as the inaugural winners of a college scholarship program named in honor of the slain pastor of Emanuel AME Church.

Applications for the Reverend Pinckney Scholars Program opened in March to black high school seniors from Beaufort, Charleston and Jasper counties. The scholarship winners also will receive supportive resources from the Coastal Community Foundation in addition to their financial awards, renewable annually for up to four years.

“This scholarship program is a wonderful tribute to my late husband,” Jennifer Pinckney, the Rev. Clementa Pinckney’s widow, said in a statement. “I am excited about getting to know the scholars through the program and helping them grow as leaders in the future.”

Following the mass killing of nine black worshippers at Emanuel AME Church last June, a group of anonymous, out-of-state donors raised more than $3 million for a college scholarship fund honoring Pinckney. More than 70 students applied. Finalists were interviewed by a selection committee that included former Charleston Mayor Joe Riley, Harvard professor Henry Louis Gates Jr. and investment banker William Lewis Jr.

The winning students are Monejah Black of Charleston Charter School for Math and Science, Altoria Brown of Garrett Academy of Technology, Christian Carter of Battery Creek High School, Brian DeLesline of Baptist Hill High School, Natrese Dorsey of North Charleston High School, Carmen Hamilton of Ridgeland-Hardeeville High School, Kayla Hasty of Ridgeland-Hardeeville High School, Henry Jones of Beaufort High School, Kierra White of R.B. Stall High School and Denzel Wright of Baptist Hill High School.
